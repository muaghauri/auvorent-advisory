Is Your Business Ready for Predictive Analytics?.

Five practical checks before investing in forecasts, dashboards or automated decision support.

Auvorent Advisory editorial · 2026-09-24 · 5 min read

1. Confirm the decision you want to improve

Forecasting should guide a real choice: hiring plans, staffing coverage, inventory, budget allocation or lead-generation capacity. Specify the decision horizon and how often managers will use the forecast.

2. Audit your data coverage

Check whether historical records are complete, consistently defined and granular enough. A model cannot reliably compensate for months of missing data, incompatible categories or inconsistent timestamps.

3. Understand what drives the outcome

Document business factors that may influence the target metric, such as seasonality, capacity limits, pricing, marketing activity, economic conditions and one-time disruptions. These may matter as much as the forecasting method.

4. Compare against a simple baseline

Begin with a transparent baseline, such as last-period performance or a seasonal average. Evaluate more sophisticated methods only when they produce better decisions, not merely more impressive charts.

5. Design a management process around uncertainty

Present ranges, assumptions and scenario sensitivities. Assign ownership for forecast review and create a process for responding when actual outcomes differ from expectations. Predictions support judgment; they do not remove uncertainty.

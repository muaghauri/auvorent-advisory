Why You Should Diagnose Before You Automate.

The practical case for identifying root causes before investing in AI and workflow automation.

Auvorent Advisory editorial · 2026-09-24 · 6 min read

Automation amplifies the process you already have

A slow workflow is not necessarily an automation problem. Delays may originate in unclear responsibilities, inaccurate intake information, conflicting approval rules or incentives that encourage rework. Adding an AI assistant before locating the real cause can make those problems harder to see rather than easier to resolve.

Start with the decision and the business outcome

Choose one measurable outcome: fewer handoff delays, faster time to response, lower rework or improved contribution margin. Trace the workflow backwards from that outcome. Interview the people performing the work, examine actual records and compare the expected process with what happens in practice.

Separate symptoms from causes

A long customer response time is a symptom. Its cause could be insufficient staffing at predictable peaks, fragmented communication, approval bottlenecks or an ineffective prioritization policy. Each cause points to a different intervention, and only some require AI.

Choose the least complex viable intervention

Eliminate unnecessary steps first. Standardize inputs and make ownership clear. Introduce deterministic automation for repetitive rules. Consider AI where language, document variability or prediction creates a strong enough business case to justify monitoring, review and operating costs.

Measure before calling it a success

Establish a baseline and a review period before implementation. Track time saved, error rates, customer outcomes and the full cost of operating the new process. Productivity improvements are useful, but they should be connected to service quality, capacity or financial outcomes.
